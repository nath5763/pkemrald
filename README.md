# Pokémon Epoch Emerald

Pokémon Epoch Emerald is a difficulty, balance, and content overhaul of Pokémon Emerald. It keeps the core Hoenn adventure while expanding the available Pokémon, modernizing battles, rebuilding major fights, adding new areas and legendary hunts, and extending the postgame.

The current project includes every major addition made since development began, not only the latest patch.

## Highlights

- Rebuilt Gym Leader, rival, villain, Elite Four, Champion, and rematch teams
- Optional Level Cap, Catch-up EXP, Team EXP Share, Random Starters, and Permadeath modes
- Smarter AI and forced Set-style rules in selected major battles
- Physical/special split and full Fairy-type support
- 18 additional Pokémon, including many Generation IV evolutions
- 37 additional moves and five additional abilities
- Expanded wild encounters featuring Pokémon from across Generations I–III
- Every legendary Pokémon from Generations I–III obtainable in one save
- New routes, towns, caves, legendary areas, a ninth Gym battle, and an expanded postgame
- Accelerated day/night cycle with outdoor palette lighting
- Field, fishing, Repel, inventory, bike, and summary-screen quality-of-life improvements

## Difficulty and Trainer Overhaul

Epoch Emerald is designed to be more demanding than the original game without relying only on higher levels.

- Gym Leaders have revised species, moves, held items, IVs, and battle formats.
- Rivals, Team Aqua and Team Magma leaders, Wally, the Elite Four, Wallace, Steven, Leaf, and other late-game opponents have upgraded teams.
- Many important battles enforce Set battle style.
- Selected bosses use upgraded AI that evaluates damage, knockout potential, survivability, switching, setup moves, stat stages, status conditions, weather, type matchups, and immunities.
- Trainer data can assign a specific ability slot to individual Pokémon, allowing important teams to use intentional strategies.
- Several major battles use modern held items and custom movesets.
- Ordinary trainers and wild encounters have been rebalanced throughout Hoenn.

### Level Cap

The optional Level Cap prevents Pokémon from gaining experience beyond the strongest Pokémon used by the next major opponent. The progression follows Roxanne, Brawly, Wattson, Flannery, Norman, Winona, Tate and Liza, Juan, Leaf, and Wallace. The cap is removed after Wallace is defeated.

Level Cap is enabled by default on new saves.

### Rematches

- All 64 ordinary Match Call trainer progressions have been rebuilt from their original Pokémon lineages.
- Original lineages evolve one stage for the first rematch and reach their final forms from the second rematch onward.
- Existing branch evolutions and rematch-only bonus Pokémon are preserved.
- Every ordinary rematch Pokémon is 10 levels higher than its previous rematch version.
- Wally's original Victory Road encounter is unchanged, while his later rematches use the new lineage rules and higher levels.
- Every Gym Leader has four rebuilt rematches:
  - Rematch 1: four Pokémon at level 70
  - Rematch 2: five Pokémon at level 75
  - Rematch 3: six Pokémon at level 80
  - Rematch 4: six Pokémon at level 85

## Configurable Gameplay Options

The redesigned Options menu scrolls and stores the following settings per save:

| Option | Effect | New-save default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Random Starters | Replaces Treecko, Torchic, and Mudkip with three distinct random Pokémon whose base-stat totals are below 320 | Off |
| Catch-up EXP | Gives progressively more experience to Pokémon that are below the current reference level | On |
| Team EXP Share | Awards experience to every otherwise eligible party member | Off |
| Level Cap | Stops each Pokémon from gaining experience beyond the current progression cap | On |
| Permadeath | Permanently retires eligible Pokémon that faint in battle | Off |

Catch-up EXP, Team EXP Share, and Level Cap are calculated separately for each party member. A capped Pokémon receives no experience without preventing a lower-level teammate from gaining experience.

When Permadeath is enabled, fainted Pokémon are removed from active use after battle and recorded in the read-only Memorial PC. The memorial preserves each retired Pokémon's nickname, species, level, and gender.

## Evolution Changes

Trade evolutions have been removed. Pokémon that previously required trading now evolve through leveling or direct item use.

Notable evolution changes include:

- Kadabra and Machoke evolve at level 40.
- Graveler evolves at level 34.
- Haunter, Onix, and Scyther evolve at level 36.
- Porygon evolves at level 25, then Porygon2 evolves into Porygon-Z at level 40.
- Poliwhirl can evolve into Politoed with a Sun Stone.
- Slowpoke can evolve into Slowking with a Water Stone.
- Seadra evolves with a Dragon Scale, without trading.
- Clamperl evolves with a Deep Sea Tooth or Deep Sea Scale, without trading.
- Feebas can evolve at level 25 as an alternative to the original Beauty requirement.
- Male Kirlia evolves into Gallade at level 30; female Kirlia evolves into Gardevoir at level 30.
- Male Snorunt evolves into Glalie at level 30; female Snorunt evolves into Froslass at level 30.
- Magneton evolves into Magnezone at level 40.
- Togetic evolves into Togekiss at level 40.
- Piloswine evolves into Mamoswine at level 40.
- Electabuzz and Magmar evolve at level 36.
- Roselia evolves into Roserade at level 36.
- Misdreavus and Murkrow evolve at level 30.
- Sneasel, Gligar, and Nosepass evolve at level 34.
- Rhydon evolves into Rhyperior at level 42.
- Budew evolves at level 12, Munchlax at level 27, and Bonsly at level 25.

Several original evolution lines also evolve earlier to improve pacing, including Ponyta, Magnemite, Omanyte, Kabuto, Trapinch, Vibrava, Sealeo, Loudred, Lairon, Lileep, and Anorith.

## Battle-System Changes

### Physical/Special Split

Move category is assigned per move instead of being determined only by type. Pokémon stats and learnsets have been adjusted around the split.

The Pokémon summary screen displays physical, special, and status categories and color-codes move names. It also color-codes stats affected by a Pokémon's nature.

### Fairy Type

Fairy is fully integrated into battle calculations, STAB, type effectiveness, Pokédex searching, summary screens, move displays, species data, and the in-game type chart.

The following families use Fairy typing where appropriate:

- Cleffa, Clefairy, and Clefable
- Igglybuff, Jigglypuff, and Wigglytuff
- Mr. Mime
- Togepi, Togetic, and Togekiss
- Azurill, Marill, and Azumarill
- Snubbull and Granbull
- Mawile
- Ralts, Kirlia, and Gardevoir

The Fairy chart follows the modern core matchups, with one project-specific rule: Fairy resists itself.

### Additional Type-Chart Changes

- Bug attacks are neutral against Fighting instead of resisted.
- Bug attacks are neutral against Ghost instead of resisted.
- Poison resists Water.

See the [type chart](output/pdf/Epoch_Emerald_Type_Chart.pdf) for the complete matchup table.

## Items and Field Tools

### Utility Items

- **Fly Tool:** A reusable Key Item that replaces the Fly HM reward after the Weather Institute rival battle and opens the normal Fly destination map.
- **Nature Candy:** Changes a Pokémon to a different random nature while preserving its gender and ability slot.
- **Ability Capsule:** Switches a Pokémon between its species' two available ability slots.

### Battle Items

- **Focus Sash:** Allows a full-health holder to survive an otherwise fatal hit.
- **Light Clay:** Extends Reflect and Light Screen from five turns to eight.
- **Choice Specs:** Boosts Special Attack but locks the holder into one move.
- **Choice Scarf:** Boosts Speed but locks the holder into one move.
- **Life Orb:** Boosts move damage and causes recoil after successful attacks.
- **Razor Claw:** Raises the holder's critical-hit ratio.

Eleven type-resistance berries have also been added for player and trainer strategies: Occa, Passho, Wacan, Rindo, Yache, Chople, Shuca, Coba, Tanga, Charti, and Colbur.

### Galadria Gym

Galadria introduces Leaf as an additional Grass-type Gym Leader. The Gym includes its own trainer sequence, puzzle progression, and a major double battle before the Elite Four.

### Legendary Hunts

Every legendary Pokémon from Generations I–III can be obtained in one save. New and revised quests use both classic event islands and new areas such as Ancient Forest, Ashen Roost, Glacier Hollow, Mt. Pyre Basement, Reservoir Cave, Scorched Cavern, Stormwake Grove, and Thunder Nest.

This includes the Kanto birds, Mewtwo and Mew, the Johto beasts, Lugia, Ho-Oh, Celebi, the Regi trio, Latias and Latios, Kyogre, Groudon, Rayquaza, Jirachi, and Deoxys.

## Expanded Postgame

- A new Champion's Club battle line unlocks after becoming Champion and defeating Steven.
- Four successive Champion's Club opponents lead to the title of Champion of Champions.
- Champion's Club Floor 2 selects four daily opponents from a rotating roster.
- The rotation includes the eight Hoenn Gym Leaders, the Elite Four, Wallace, Steven, Leaf, Brendan, and May.
- Daily victories are tracked separately and reset when the in-game day changes.
- Existing postgame fights, maps, scripts, and rewards have been revised.

## Day and Night

- The in-game clock runs at six times real-world speed.
- Outdoor towns, cities, routes, and ocean routes use dawn, day, dusk, and night palettes.
- Lighting transitions gradually around dawn and dusk instead of changing abruptly.
- Weather and overworld sprite palettes remain integrated with the time-of-day tint.

The current system changes palette lighting; it does not add separate nighttime tiles, illuminated windows, or streetlights.

## Quality-of-Life Changes

- Repel expiration offers to use another Repel of the same type.
- Max Repel lasts 500 steps.
- The bike can switch between Mach and Acro modes while riding.
- Running is allowed on maps that originally disabled it, while metatile-specific restrictions remain respected.
- Fishing always produces a bite on maps with fishing encounters, and the reel prompt waits until the player presses A.
- Field items, hidden items, and scripted rewards are transferred to the PC when the Bag is full and PC space is available.
- The Fly HM reward has been replaced by the reusable Fly Tool.
- A Leftovers pickup has been added to Route 135.
- The move summary shows physical, special, and status categories with distinct colors.
- Nature-raised and nature-lowered stats are color-coded on the summary screen.
- Encounter tables, healing locations, item placements, story flags, and event scripts have received numerous fixes and pacing adjustments.

## Download and Installation

Download the latest UPS patch from the [GitHub Releases page](https://github.com/nath5763/pkemrald/releases/).

Apply the patch to a clean Pokémon Emerald (USA) ROM with this SHA-1 checksum:

```text
f3ae088181bf583e55daf962a92bb46f4f1d07b7
```

Use a UPS-compatible patcher such as NUPS. Distribute the UPS patch, not a pre-patched ROM. You must provide your own legally obtained base ROM.

Because the project changes progression, encounters, trainer data, species data, and save-backed options, starting a new save is recommended. Very old and pre-1.0 saves may not be compatible.

## Building from Source

This project is based on the pret Pokémon Emerald decompilation and builds `pokeemerald.gba`.

Follow [INSTALL.md](INSTALL.md) to install the toolchain, then build with:

```sh
make -j4
```

On the project's Windows/WSL setup, the equivalent command is:

```sh
wsl make -j4
```
